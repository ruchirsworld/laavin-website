Balcony Gardens: A Practical Guide for Apartments

Laavin Infrascapes11 Oct 20265 min read

A balcony is often the only outdoor space an apartment has, and a few well-chosen plants can make it the favourite spot in the home. Balconies are small, exposed and shared with neighbours below, so a good balcony garden is planned around light, weight and water from the start.

1. Read the light first

The direction a balcony faces decides what will grow. An east-facing balcony gets gentle morning sun, a north-facing one stays bright but mostly shaded, and south and west faces get strong afternoon heat in a Delhi summer. Watch your balcony for a day and note how many hours of direct sun it gets before choosing plants.

2. Respect the weight and the floor

Wet soil and large pots are heavier than they look. Keep the heaviest containers near the wall or the edge supported by the building, not in the middle of a cantilevered slab. Raise pots on feet or trays so water can drain and the balcony floor stays dry, and check your society's rules before fixing anything to walls or railings.

3. Make sure water drains the right way

Water dripping onto the balcony below is the fastest way to upset a neighbour. Use pots with drainage holes, sit them in saucers or trays, and make sure the balcony floor drains to its outlet rather than over the edge.

On a balcony, the best garden is one your neighbours never notice: no drips, no falling pots, no leaves blowing onto the floor below.

4. Pick the right containers

Lighter containers, such as good-quality plastic, fibreglass or lightweight composite, keep the load down. Fill them with a light potting mix rather than garden soil. Railing planters should be fixed securely, especially on higher floors where the wind is stronger.

5. Choose plants for your light

  • Full sun: bougainvillea, hibiscus, succulents, and herbs such as tulsi and lemongrass.
  • Part sun: money plant, spider plant, syngonium and many flowering annuals in winter.
  • Shade: ferns, peace lily and other foliage plants that prefer softer light.

6. Keep watering simple

Small pots dry out quickly in summer and need far less water in winter. A small drip kit on a timer, or self-watering pots, keeps plants healthy when you travel and stops the overwatering that rots roots.

7. Plan for wind and birds

Higher floors catch more wind, so choose sturdy, low plants for exposed balconies and avoid tall pots near the railing. Pigeons are a common problem in many apartment blocks; bird netting keeps the garden and the floor clean.
