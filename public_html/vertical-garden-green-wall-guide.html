Vertical Gardens and Green Walls: How to Keep Them Alive

Laavin Infrascapes11 Oct 20266 min read

A green wall can turn a bare facade, a lobby or a boundary wall into the most memorable part of a building. It is also one of the easiest landscapes to get wrong. A vertical garden has very little soil and no room for error, so it lives or dies by its system, its water and its light.

1. Choose the right system

There are a few common ways to build a vertical garden, and each suits a different site and budget:

  • Modular panels or trays: plants grow in pre-planted units that clip onto a frame. Easy to replace one section at a time.
  • Felt or pocket systems: plants sit in fabric pockets fixed to a waterproof backing. Light and flexible, but they need very reliable watering.
  • Green facades: climbers grow up wires or a trellis from planters at the base. The simplest and lowest-maintenance option for large outdoor walls, though slower to fill in.

2. Protect the wall behind it

A green wall keeps a lot of moisture against a building. The system should stand slightly off the wall on a frame, with a waterproof backing between the plants and the structure, so water never soaks into plaster or brickwork. The frame and fixings need to carry the full wet weight of the panels.

3. Irrigation is not optional

With so little growing medium, a vertical garden dries out within days if watering fails. It needs drip irrigation on a timer, emitters spread so every part of the wall gets water, and a gutter or tray at the base to catch the runoff. On larger walls, splitting the system into zones helps, because the top dries faster than the bottom.

Most failed green walls die of water problems: a blocked emitter, a timer that stopped, or a dry patch nobody noticed. Plan the irrigation as carefully as the planting.

4. Match the plants to the light

Light decides which plants will survive. Outdoor walls in full Delhi sun need hardy, heat-tolerant species, while shaded walls can take ferns and foliage plants. Indoors, most walls are darker than they look, so choose plants that tolerate low light, such as money plant, philodendron, syngonium and peace lily, or add grow lights where daylight is weak.

5. Think about the weather

Outdoor walls face hot dry winds in May and June and heavy rain in the monsoon. Plan for more frequent watering in summer, check that drainage copes with downpours, and avoid placing tender plants on the most exposed sides.

6. Plan the upkeep

  • Trim plants regularly so the wall stays even and nothing smothers its neighbours.
  • Check emitters and the timer often, and clean filters so lines do not clog.
  • Feed through the irrigation or by hand, because the medium holds few nutrients.
  • Replace weak plants quickly, before gaps spread.
